# Groucho background model comparison — 8 November 2026

Qwen3 235B Instruct is worth a larger profile-extraction trial. Mistral Small 3.2 was cheapest but introduced more attribution and taxonomy errors. Neither is ready to replace cultural-signal extraction on this evidence. Claude also showed quality gaps; model substitution alone will not resolve the shared profile-privacy and correction-contract issues.

No live application model, prompt, data or behaviour was changed.

## What ran

72 paid API calls: six authored synthetic conversations × two tasks × three models × two repeats. Tasks were profile extraction (including summary and reconstructed Q&A) and completed-session cultural-signal extraction. Actual current production prompts, schemas and output token limits were captured by intercepting the Anthropic client; database access was blocked. All models received identical task inputs and schemas, translated into OpenRouter's structured-output format, with temperature zero.

Models:

- Claude Haiku 4.5: `anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5`, matching the source default for these background tasks.
- Mistral Small 3.2 24B: `mistralai/mistral-small-3.2-24b-instruct`.
- Qwen3 235B A22B Instruct 2507: `qwen/qwen3-235b-a22b-2507`.

These are specific versions, not a comparison of each vendor's newest or best model. Deployed environment overrides were not audited. This trial does not cover eligibility/reviewer reports, custom persona fields, long transcripts or live conversations.

OpenRouter served Claude through Amazon Bedrock (24 calls), Qwen through Parasail (24), and Mistral through DeepInfra (14) and Mistral (10). Provider selection, implementation and caching are therefore confounders; the test measures these served configurations, not model weights in isolation. No EU-residency conclusion can be drawn.

## Measured costs and latency

Costs below are `usage.cost` returned by the API, including its reported cache effects. All 72 calls returned costs. Total inference charge reported: **$0.038460815** (about 3.85 US cents), excluding credit-purchase fees and engineering/hosting overhead.

| Model | Profile: USD per 1,000 calls | Cultural signals: USD per 1,000 calls | Both tasks: USD per 1,000 pairs | Median profile latency | Median cultural latency |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Claude Haiku 4.5 | $1.710 | $1.092 | $2.802 | 1.43 s | 1.24 s |
| Mistral Small 3.2 | $0.066 | $0.037 | $0.103 | 2.24 s | 1.19 s |
| Qwen3 235B Instruct | $0.207 | $0.093 | $0.300 | 5.65 s | 2.91 s |

For this input mix, paired-task inference was about **96.3% cheaper with Mistral** and **89.3% cheaper with Qwen**. Qwen profile extraction alone was about **87.9% cheaper**, with approximately four times Claude's median response time. These are linear extrapolations of short synthetic cases, not a forecast for production transcripts. They do not establish savings against other Claude versions or the direct Anthropic API.

Every call returned complete JSON that passed the production schema and tested field limits: **24/24 per model**. There were no HTTP failures, application retries or truncations. Structural success did not imply semantic success.

## Quality findings

The coding assistant read all six complete synthetic conversations and all 72 raw outputs. This is qualitative review, not a blinded human study. Repeats are correlated, and no statistical superiority claim is warranted. Each output has an assessment and explanation in the review artifact.

| Task and model | Pass | Minor issue | Material failure | Shared contract gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Profile — Claude | 8 | 2 | 0 | 2 |
| Profile — Mistral | 6 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Profile — Qwen | 10 | 0 | 0 | 2 |
| Cultural — Claude | 8 | 2 | 0 | 2 |
| Cultural — Mistral | 5 | 2 | 5 | 0 |
| Cultural — Qwen | 7 | 2 | 3 | 0 |

A contract gap is not a pass. If a result contains both a concrete error and a contract gap, the table records the concrete error and the written note preserves both findings. Categories are judgement calls explained in the artifact, not an automatic release score.

- **Correction handling:** Qwen's profiles clearly retained beginner photography and monthly availability, without attributing the brother's experience to the applicant. Claude retained event organisation as an interest; Mistral once converted willingness to help into an existing habit of assisting events.
- **Assistant attribution:** All models correctly retained ceramics and folk rather than the assistant's suggestions of DJing, techno and Radiohead, and avoided inventing teaching experience.
- **Injection:** Claude and Qwen profiles treated the embedded command as a test instruction. Mistral's first summary said the applicant insisted on expert event-organising experience; its second output added event organising to interests. Both labelled the applicant hostile without sufficient tonal evidence.
- **Cultural taxonomy:** Mistral labelled illustrators, ambient music and film photography as artist references in different outputs. Qwen classified ambient music as an artist once, then correctly as a genre on its repeat.
- **Coverage:** Qwen lost the legitimate weaving signal in both injection-case runs; Mistral lost it once. Claude retained weaving but omitted Nina Simone from both French cultural extractions. Mistral and Qwen retained Nina Simone.
- **Language:** All profile models correctly identified French and preserved the main French facts. Some summaries were French and others English; the current prompt does not prescribe one output language.

## Shared product issues exposed

All three models correctly emitted empty cultural signals for the sensitive-only case. However, all three profile summaries repeated volunteered religion and health details. Claude also repeated the address. The current profile prompt does not explicitly prohibit those details. Its existing post-processing redacts email addresses and phone-like strings, but not religion or health. Raw Q&A outputs also reproduced the supplied personal information; email is subsequently redacted by production normalisation.

The cultural prompt says to extract explicit applicant-message signals but does not specify how later corrections supersede earlier statements. Claude and Mistral retained withdrawn music-production/event references. Qwen reconciled the correction more successfully, but that behaviour is not guaranteed by the contract. A final applicant-interest view needs a clear correction policy, distinct from per-message mention extraction.

Conversation-quality implication: a model change could lower cost while silently distorting the applicant's intent, attribution or interests. Preserve full-conversation review alongside mechanical checks. This benchmark changed no production behaviour and does not authorise rollout.

## Recommendation

Trial **Qwen for profile extraction in shadow mode**, after clarifying sensitive-data minimisation. Expand to longer synthetic or appropriately approved/redacted transcripts, custom fields and representative edge cases; pin the intended provider and compare against the actually deployed Claude model. Review outputs before considering a switch.

Keep current cultural extraction unchanged for now. Clarify correction handling and strengthen taxonomy/coverage evaluation, then repeat the comparison. Mistral remains a cost candidate but needs more work before use in applicant profiles or cultural aggregation. Do not extrapolate these results to reviewer/eligibility decisions.

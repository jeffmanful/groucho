## Minimum screen and interaction requirements

1. **Event setup:** registration window, quantity, available opportunities and
   verification policy. Dan can change a welcome or policy label and show the
   matching front-end change. Configuration cannot alter an already frozen draw.
2. **Fan registration:** short explanation, verification states and optional
   conversation. Preserve input on failure. Show which checks are simulated.
3. **Registration list:** email verification, social-control state, concrete flags,
   review state and eligibility. Filters are views, not exclusion actions.
4. **Evidence card:** verified/observed/claimed/unknown sections, reason/source,
   related registrations and a review action with an audit entry. Avoid a single
   “human score.” No penalty for private social history or missing prior purchases.
5. **Cohort and allocation:** named policy, eligible count, excluded/pending counts
   with reasons, six opportunities, snapshot confirmation, draw ID and results.
6. **Result:** winner/waitlist view and labelled simulated entitlement handoff.
   Use “purchase opportunity,” not “ticket issued.”

For this one-ticket-per-entry demo, every eligible registration has one equal
chance. Prevent duplicate records entering the draw, and select without replacement.
Freeze participant IDs, policy, time and draw result. Use a cryptographically
secure shuffle/selection if a real random draw is implemented; an audit log alone
does not make a lottery independently verifiable. Never rig a live draw to produce
a preferred outcome. A deterministic fixture must be labelled a rehearsal result.
Block repeat allocation to the same snapshot; an explicit reset starts a new demo
run and preserves the distinction from production audit requirements.

## Ready-to-demo acceptance checks

- Dan can sign in, operate the flow and reset it without developer intervention.
- The demo cannot expose or change existing client applications; only synthetic
  data is available. Simulation mode cannot authorise production operations.
- A fan submission appears once in the dashboard; retries do not duplicate it.
- Every displayed flag has an inspectable reason and an explicit synthetic source.
- Holding/releasing a record updates eligibility visibly and records the action;
  a list filter alone never changes eligibility.
- An unresolved duplicate cannot enter the cohort twice. Private/no-social cases
  can use the stated alternative policy rather than automatic rejection.
- The preview count matches the frozen input. Six distinct eligible entries win,
  remaining entries are accounted for, and pending/held entries do not win.
- A repeat draw click does not create extra opportunities. Reset creates an
  unmistakably new demo run.
- All provider, history, notification and ticketing simulations are labelled.
- Core pages work on the presentation device, keyboard and expected viewport.
  Basic loading, error and retry states work.
- Run focused meaningful checks for eligibility, duplicate handling and draw
  invariants, plus required project checks appropriate to the changed code. Record
  results and known limitations before rehearsal; no tests are claimed by this plan.

## Questions for Fi

Ask about her current process before pitching specific vendor checks:

1. Which stage causes the greatest loss or manual work, and what does a bad sale
   look like operationally?
2. What registration evidence is available now, and who makes shortlist decisions?
3. Would this dashboard remove meaningful work? What would it need to export or
   integrate with to be useful?
4. Who controls presale codes, inventory and buyer binding? Who should validate
   feasibility on the ticketing side?
5. Which fan checks would be acceptable, and who would support failures/exceptions?
6. Would lotteries/cohorts fit actual artist/promoter policies? What would feel
   unfair or exclude genuine fans?
7. Is there a small presale where this could be tested, and what measurable result
   would justify continuing?

Record confirmed problems, unsupported assumptions, available data/integrations,
decision owners and any volunteered next step. Seek permission before attributing
feedback externally. A useful outcome may be a narrower pilot or a decision that
this workflow is not the primary problem.
